Q1. k-Means minimises:

  • A. The within-cluster sum of squared distances to centroids (WCSS)
  • B. The number of clusters
  • C. The silhouette
  • D. The likelihood of a Gaussian mixture
Answer A. \(J=\sum_j\sum_{x\in C_j}\|x-\mu_j\|^2\).

Q2. Why does Lloyd's algorithm always converge?

  • A. It finds the global optimum
  • B. k is fixed
  • C. It uses gradient descent with momentum
  • D. Neither the assignment nor the update step can increase J, which is bounded below
Answer D. Monotone decrease to a local minimum; restarts and k-means++ reduce poor minima.

Q3. WCSS keeps decreasing as k increases. Therefore:

  • A. Choose the largest k
  • B. WCSS is useless
  • C. Choose k at the elbow and confirm with silhouette/domain knowledge
  • D. Always choose k = 2
Answer C. WCSS is zero when k = N; look for diminishing returns (k = 5 here).

Q4. A point has silhouette \(s(i)=-0.2\). This means:

  • A. It is well clustered
  • B. It is noise
  • C. It is closer on average to another cluster than to its own: probably misassigned
  • D. The clustering is perfect
Answer C. \(s<0\iff b(i)

Q5. The INFILTRATION archetype was significant because:

  • A. It was not in the analysts' label scheme: a tactic discovered without labels
  • B. It was the largest cluster
  • C. It had the highest speed
  • D. DBSCAN labelled it noise
Answer A. Unsupervised learning surfaces categories nobody had defined; experts then validated it.

Q6. In DBSCAN, a border point is:

  • A. A point with at least MinPts neighbours
  • B. A non-core point within epsilon of a core point
  • C. An outlier
  • D. A centroid
Answer B. Core: at least MinPts within epsilon; border: reachable from a core point; noise: neither.

Q7. DBSCAN's 142 noise trajectories were interpreted as:

  • A. Sensor errors to discard
  • B. Patrols
  • C. Deliberate deception manoeuvres
  • D. Duplicate records
Answer C. In defence, 'noise' is often the most interesting signal; k-Means would have hidden them.

Q8. Increasing MinPts from 8 to 15 (same epsilon) will:

  • A. Increase the number of noise points (318 vs 142)
  • B. Reduce the number of noise points
  • C. Have no effect
  • D. Merge all clusters
Answer A. A stricter density requirement makes more points non-core.

Q9. GMM responsibilities \(r_{ik}\) are:

  • A. Hard 0/1 assignments
  • B. Distances
  • C. Cluster sizes
  • D. Posterior probabilities that point i came from component k
Answer D. Soft membership: e.g. T481 had 0.62 assault / 0.38 infiltration.

Q10. For selecting the number of GMM components you should use:

  • A. The raw log-likelihood
  • B. Training time
  • C. The number of features
  • D. AIC or BIC, which penalise extra parameters
Answer D. Likelihood always rises with K; AIC/BIC both chose K = 5.

Q11. Ward linkage merges the pair of clusters that:

  • A. Are farthest apart
  • B. Have the most points
  • C. Least increases the total within-cluster variance
  • D. Are nearest by single linkage
Answer C. It produces compact clusters and suits defence analysis.

Q12. ARI = 0.847 between Ward and k-Means means:

  • A. Two different methods produced very similar partitions: evidence the clusters are real
  • B. The methods disagree
  • C. The data are noise
  • D. k is wrong
Answer A. ARI is 1 for identical partitions and about 0 for chance agreement.

Q13. PCA's first principal component is:

  • A. The feature with the largest mean
  • B. The eigenvector of the covariance matrix with the largest eigenvalue
  • C. A random direction
  • D. The cluster centroid
Answer B. It maximises projected variance; its eigenvalue is that variance.

Q14. Four PCs with eigenvalues 4.21, 2.84, 1.62, 1.45 out of a total of 12 explain about:

  • A. 35%
  • B. 59%
  • C. 84%
  • D. 100%
Answer C. \((4.21+2.84+1.62+1.45)/12=10.12/12\approx0.84\).

Q15. In a t-SNE plot, which statement is correct?

  • A. Distances between clusters are meaningful
  • B. t-SNE is linear
  • C. Cluster sizes show true densities
  • D. Only local neighbourhood structure is reliably preserved
Answer D. Never interpret between-cluster distances or sizes in t-SNE; UMAP preserves somewhat more global structure.

Part B — Assignment

Instructions. Five problems, 100 marks, plus a 20-mark bonus problem (Problem 6). Show all working. Reference: Lecture 4 reference text.

Problem 1: k-Means by hand 20 marks

One-dimensional speeds: 1, 2, 3, 10, 11, 12, 20. Run $k$-Means with $k=2$ and initial centroids 1 and 20.

(a) Perform iterations until convergence, showing assignments and centroids. [10]
(b) Compute the final WCSS. [5]
(c) What would $k=3$ give, and how would you decide between 2 and 3? [5]

Show full solution

(a) Iteration 1: $\{1,2,3,10\}$ (10 is 9 from 1 and 10 from 20) and $\{11,12,20\}$; centroids 4 and 14.33. Iteration 2: 10 is now 6 from 4 and 4.33 from 14.33, so it moves: $\{1,2,3\}$, $\{10,11,12,20\}$; centroids 2 and 13.25. Iteration 3: no change → converged.

(b) Cluster 1: $1+0+1=2$. Cluster 2: $3.25^2+2.25^2+1.25^2+6.75^2=10.56+5.06+1.56+45.56=62.75$. $J=\mathbf{64.75}$.

(c) $k=3$ gives $\{1,2,3\}$, $\{10,11,12\}$, $\{20\}$ with $J=2+2+0=4$, a huge drop. Since WCSS always falls with $k$, judge by the size of the drop, silhouette, and meaning: a singleton cluster may be an outlier (DBSCAN would call it noise) rather than a real group.

Problem 2: Silhouette values 15 marks

Point P is in cluster A; its distances to the other members of A are 1 and 2; to the members of the nearest other cluster B they are 4, 5 and 6. Point Q in cluster A has mean distance 3 to A and 2.5 to B.

(a) Compute $s(P)$ and $s(Q)$. [8]
(b) Interpret both and state what you would do about Q. [7]

Show full solution

(a) $a(P)=1.5$, $b(P)=5$, $s(P)=(5-1.5)/5=\mathbf{0.70}$. $s(Q)=(2.5-3)/3=\mathbf{-0.17}$.

(b) P is well clustered. Q is closer on average to B than to its own cluster: probably misassigned, or a boundary/ambiguous case. Reassign it, examine it as a possible anomaly, or use a soft method (GMM) to report its membership probabilities.

Problem 3: One EM iteration 25 marks

A two-component 1-D GMM has $\pi_1=\pi_2=0.5$, $\mu_1=0$, $\mu_2=4$, $\sigma_1=\sigma_2=1$. Data: $x=0, 1.5, 4$.

(a) E-step: compute the responsibilities for all three points. [12]
(b) M-step: update $\pi_1$ and $\mu_1$. [8]
(c) Explain why the likelihood cannot decrease, and name two practical pitfalls of EM. [5]

Show full solution

(a) $\mathcal N(x;\mu,1)=0.3989e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2}$. $x=0$: $0.3989$ vs $0.3989e^{-8}=1.34\times10^{-4}$; $r_1=\mathbf{0.9997}$. $x=1.5$: $0.3989e^{-1.125}=0.1295$ vs $0.3989e^{-3.125}=0.0175$; $r_1=0.1295/0.1470=\mathbf{0.881}$, $r_2=0.119$. $x=4$: symmetric to $x=0$: $r_1=\mathbf{0.0003}$.

(b) $N_1=0.9997+0.881+0.0003=1.881$; $\pi_1=1.881/3=\mathbf{0.627}$; $\mu_1=(0.9997\cdot0+0.881\cdot1.5+0.0003\cdot4)/1.881=1.323/1.881=\mathbf{0.703}$.

(c) EM maximises a lower bound on the log-likelihood that touches it at the current parameters (E-step) and then raises the bound (M-step), so the likelihood never falls. Pitfalls: local maxima (use multiple starts), collapsing components with $\sigma\to0$ (regularise), label switching, and sensitivity to initialisation.

Problem 4: PCA by hand 20 marks

Two standardised features (speed, direction change) have covariance matrix $C=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}$.

(a) Find the eigenvalues and unit eigenvectors. [10]
(b) Compute each component's explained-variance ratio. [4]
(c) Project the point $(2,0)$ onto PC1 and interpret PC1. [6]

Show full solution

(a) $\det(C-\lambda I)=(3-\lambda)^2-1=0\Rightarrow\lambda=\mathbf{4},\mathbf{2}$. For $\lambda=4$: $(1,1)/\sqrt2$; for $\lambda=2$: $(1,-1)/\sqrt2$.

(b) EVR $=4/6=\mathbf{66.7\%}$ and $2/6=\mathbf{33.3\%}$.

(c) Score $=(2,0)\cdot(1,1)/\sqrt2=\mathbf{1.41}$. PC1 weights speed and direction change equally: an overall 'activity' axis on which fast, manoeuvring trajectories score high; PC2 contrasts the two (fast but straight vs slow but erratic).

Problem 5: DBSCAN by hand 20 marks

Points: 1, 2, 3, 4, 10, 11, 12, 20 with $\varepsilon=1.5$ and MinPts = 3 (a point counts itself).

(a) Label each point core, border or noise and give the clusters. [10]
(b) Repeat with MinPts = 4. [5]
(c) Explain why the noise point might matter more than either cluster in an intelligence context. [5]

Show full solution

(a) Neighbourhoods: 1: $\{1,2\}$; 2: $\{1,2,3\}$; 3: $\{2,3,4\}$; 4: $\{3,4\}$; 10: $\{10,11\}$; 11: $\{10,11,12\}$; 12: $\{11,12\}$; 20: $\{20\}$. Core: 2, 3, 11. Border: 1, 4 (near 2 and 3), 10, 12 (near 11). Noise: 20. Clusters $\{1,2,3,4\}$ and $\{10,11,12\}$.

(b) No point has 4 neighbours within 1.5, so there are no core points and everything is noise: MinPts too large for this density.

(c) An isolated behaviour unlike any routine pattern can be a new tactic, deception or a sensor fault. DBSCAN surfaces it explicitly for analysts, whereas $k$-Means would absorb it into the nearest group (as with the 142 deception manoeuvres).

Problem 6 (bonus): Discovering emitter families 20 marks

Nine radar intercepts are described by pulse repetition frequency (PRF, 800–3,000 Hz) and pulse width (0.5–5 µs). After standardisation their coordinates are: P1 $(0,0)$, P2 $(0.4,0.2)$, P3 $(0.2,0.5)$, P4 $(0.5,0.6)$, P5 $(3,3)$, P6 $(3.3,2.8)$, P7 $(3.1,3.4)$, P8 $(1.6,1.7)$, P9 $(5,0)$.

(a) Why must the raw features be standardised before clustering? [3]
(b) Run DBSCAN with $\varepsilon=0.6$ and MinPts $=3$ (counting the point itself). Label every point core, border or noise and give the clusters. [7]
(c) Compute the silhouette of P4, taking clusters A $=\{$P1–P4$\}$ and B $=\{$P5–P7$\}$. [4]
(d) Along the PRF axis alone, a two-component mixture has family A $\sim\mathcal N(1.2,0.3^2)$ with weight 0.6 and family B $\sim\mathcal N(2.4,0.4^2)$ with weight 0.4 (standardised units). Compute the responsibility of family A for an intercept at 1.8, and say what an analyst should do with it. [6]

Show full solution

(a) Raw PRF differences run to thousands of hertz while pulse-width differences are a few microseconds, so Euclidean distance would be decided almost entirely by PRF. Standardising each feature to zero mean and unit variance gives both a fair say. Record the means and standard deviations, because new intercepts must be transformed the same way.

(b) Neighbour counts within 0.6: P1 3, P2 4, P3 4, P4 3, P5 3 ⇒ core. P6 2 and P7 2, but each lies within 0.6 of the core point P5 ⇒ border (P6–P7 is 0.63, just outside). P8 and P9 have only themselves ⇒ noise. Clusters: A $=\{$P1, P2, P3, P4$\}$; B $=\{$P5, P6, P7$\}$. P8 lies between the families (mean distance 1.91 to A, 2.07 to B); $k$-means would force it into A, whereas DBSCAN leaves it for an analyst. P9 is isolated: a candidate new emitter type.

(c) $a$ = mean distance from P4 to P1, P2, P3 $=0.50$; $b$ = mean distance to P5, P6, P7 $=3.62$; $s=(3.62-0.50)/3.62=\mathbf{0.86}$: firmly inside its cluster.

(d) $0.6\,\mathcal N(1.8;1.2,0.3^2)=0.108$ and $0.4\,\mathcal N(1.8;2.4,0.4^2)=0.130$, so $r_A=0.108/0.238=\mathbf{0.45}$. The intercept is genuinely ambiguous (an intercept at 2.0 would give 0.09). Report it as uncertain and seek a second feature (pulse width, scan pattern) or a further intercept rather than forcing a label; soft assignments are exactly what a GMM is for.
