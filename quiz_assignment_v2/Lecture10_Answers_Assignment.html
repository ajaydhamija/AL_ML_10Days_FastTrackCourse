Q1. Which problem statement is strongest?

  • A. We applied ML to air defence
  • B. Our model is very accurate
  • C. We used a deep network
  • D. IADS operators get 400 tracks/hour and need >=95% accuracy with bomber recall >95% in <=3 ms
Answer D. Specific, quantified, operational, with a clear decision supported.

Q2. A results slide must include at least:

  • A. A baseline comparison, the appropriate per-class or task metrics and uncertainty
  • B. Only accuracy
  • C. The training loss
  • D. The code
Answer A. 87% means nothing without context.

Q3. Your accuracy is 95% on 400 test cases. The approximate 95% CI half-width is:

  • A. 0.2%
  • B. 2.1%
  • C. 5%
  • D. 10%
Answer B. \(1.96\sqrt{0.95\times0.05/400}=0.021\).

Q4. To compare two classifiers on the same test set you should use:

  • A. Two independent CIs only
  • B. A t-test on training loss
  • C. McNemar's test on paired disagreements
  • D. Nothing
Answer C. Paired predictions require a paired test.

Q5. For RL projects, results should be reported:

  • A. As mean and spread over several seeds and opponents
  • B. From the single best seed
  • C. As training reward only
  • D. Without baselines
Answer A. RL is high-variance; at least five seeds.

Q6. An ablation study:

  • A. Adds features
  • B. Removes one component at a time to show what drives performance
  • C. Tunes the learning rate
  • D. Is optional decoration
Answer B. It shows which parts of the system matter.

Q7. Which limitation statement is best?

  • A. Future work could improve accuracy
  • B. Trained only on L-band signatures; frequency-agile X-band radars would need retraining
  • C. There may be bugs
  • D. More data would help
Answer B. Specific and testable.

Q8. STARE stands for:

  • A. Search, Test, Analyse, Report, Evaluate
  • B. Simulate, Tune, Apply, Retrain, Explain
  • C. Start, Train, Assess, Run, Exit
  • D. State, Tie, Acknowledge, Reason, End
Answer D. A structured, honest way to answer hard questions.

Q9. When you do not know an answer, the panel prefers that you:

  • A. Bluff confidently
  • B. Change the subject
  • C. Acknowledge the gap honestly and reason about what would happen
  • D. Cite an unrelated result
Answer C. Honesty is rewarded; bluffing is detected.

Q10. Which rubric criterion carries the highest weight?

  • A. Problem definition
  • B. Algorithm justification and results/validation (25% each)
  • C. Ethics
  • D. Peer evaluation
Answer B. 20/25/25/20/10.

Q11. Claiming 'ready for deployment' from simulation results is:

  • A. Acceptable
  • B. Required
  • C. A common pitfall; the sim-to-real gap must be estimated and closed
  • D. Only wrong for RL
Answer C. Drones fell 94% to 78% without randomisation.

Q12. Peer evaluation contributes:

  • A. 0%
  • B. 100%
  • C. 50%
  • D. 20% of the project grade
Answer D. Moderated by the instructor.

Q13. A deployment plan should include:

  • A. Autonomy level, latency, interface, ROE/IHL compliance, sim-to-real gap and monitoring
  • B. Only accuracy
  • C. The dataset size only
  • D. Marketing claims
Answer A. This separates operationally mature projects from merely competent ones.

Q14. A trivial baseline for an imbalanced classifier is:

  • A. Random forest
  • B. A deep network
  • C. Always predicting the majority class
  • D. SVM
Answer C. Shows how much of the accuracy is just class frequency.

Q15. Why split sequential data by mission or day rather than randomly?

  • A. To save time
  • B. To prevent temporal leakage between correlated samples in train and test
  • C. It is required by law
  • D. To balance classes
Answer B. Random splits put near-duplicates on both sides.

Part B — Assignment

Instructions. This assignment rehearses your project. 100 marks. Model answers are provided for self-assessment.

Problem 1: Project proposal 40 marks

Choose one catalogue topic (T1–T8) and write a one-page proposal: problem statement with numbers; data and its pitfalls; candidate methods with at least two alternatives; evaluation protocol with baselines and uncertainty; deployment concept; two specific limitations.

Show full solution

Model answer (T4, cyber anomaly): Problem: a brigade network generates 2M events/day; analysts can triage 200 alerts; the signature IDS misses novel attacks. Data: 30 days of normal traffic plus red-team attacks; split by day; base rate about 0.01%; possible survivorship (only detected incidents labelled) and drift. Methods: Isolation Forest screen + autoencoder confirmation; alternatives: z-score/Mahalanobis, one-class SVM. Evaluation: TPR at a fixed 150 false alerts/day; precision at the base rate; bootstrap CIs; per-attack recall; ablation without tier 1; a held-out novel attack type. Deployment: edge server, Level-2 containment approvals, drift monitoring, monthly retraining. Limitations: mimicry attacks imitating normal traffic; red-team traffic may not represent real adversaries.

Problem 2: Results with uncertainty 30 marks

Your classifier scores 93.0% on 500 test tracks; a baseline scores 91.0% on the same tracks. Of the tracks on which they disagree, yours is right on 34 and the baseline on 24.

(a) Compute 95% CIs for both accuracies. [10]
(b) Apply McNemar's test. [10]
(c) Write the results sentence you would put on your slide. [10]

Show full solution

(a) Yours: $1.96\sqrt{0.93(0.07)/500}=2.2\%$ → (90.8, 95.2). Baseline: $1.96\sqrt{0.91(0.09)/500}=2.5\%$ → (88.5, 93.5). The intervals overlap.

(b) $b=34$, $c=24$: $\chi^2=(34-24)^2/58=1.72$; $p\approx0.19$. Not significant.

(c) ``Our classifier achieved 93.0% (95% CI 90.8–95.2) versus 91.0% for the baseline on the same 500 tracks; the difference is not statistically significant (McNemar $p=0.19$), so a larger test set or deployment-domain data is needed to establish an advantage.''

Problem 3: Rehearsing the panel 30 marks

Write STARE answers to three likely panel questions for your project: one on data bias, one on false-alarm cost, and one on autonomy and accountability.

Show full solution

Data bias. S: trained on 30 days of traffic from two segments. T: slide 3 lists the sources. A: no data from deployed forward networks. R: those networks may show different protocols, so the FPR may rise. E: collect a 2-week sample from the target network and re-calibrate the threshold.

False-alarm cost. S: 150 false alerts/day at 87% TPR. T: slide 6 trade-off curve. A: analyst time per alert is assumed, not measured. R: if triage takes longer than 2 min, capacity is exceeded. E: measure triage time and add per-host aggregation.

Autonomy. S: the system recommends isolation of hosts. T: slide 8 interface mock-up. A: automatic isolation was not tested for collateral impact. R: false isolation could cut critical services. E: keep Level 2 (analyst approval), with full logging and a rollback procedure.
