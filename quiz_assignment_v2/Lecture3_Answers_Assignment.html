Q1. The OLS solution is:

  • A. \(w^*=X^\top y\)
  • B. \(w^*=(XX^\top)^{-1}y\)
  • C. \(w^*=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y\)
  • D. \(w^*=X^{-1}y\)
Answer C. Setting \(\nabla\|y-Xw\|^2=0\) gives the normal equations \(X^\top Xw=X^\top y\).

Q2. A Poisson casualty model has \(\ln\mu=0.5+0.25x\), where \(x\) is the number of enemy artillery battalions in range. Each additional battalion multiplies the expected casualties by about:

  • A. 1.28
  • B. 1.65
  • C. 2.72
  • D. 4.48
Answer A. The rate ratio is \(e^{0.25}=1.28\): a 28% increase per battalion. (1.65, 2.72 and 4.48 are the expected counts at 0, 2 and 4 battalions.)

Q3. A VIF of 8.4 for force size indicates:

  • A. No problem
  • B. Strong collinearity inflating that coefficient's variance
  • C. A non-linear effect
  • D. An outlier
Answer B. \(\text{VIF}=1/(1-R_j^2)\); values above about 5--10 are a warning; ridge reduced it to 2.1.

Q4. Ridge regression differs from lasso because ridge:

  • A. Sets some coefficients exactly to zero
  • B. Shrinks all coefficients smoothly but none to exactly zero
  • C. Has no closed form
  • D. Uses an absolute-value penalty
Answer B. In 1-D ridge gives \(z/(1+\lambda)\); lasso soft-thresholds to exact zeros.

Q5. Why does the L1 penalty produce exact zeros?

  • A. It is quadratic
  • B. It is differentiable everywhere
  • C. Its constraint region has corners on the axes where the loss contours tend to touch
  • D. It increases variance
Answer C. The diamond-shaped region meets the ellipses at corners, where some \(w_j=0\).

Q6. In Case A, lasso (lambda = 0.3) zeroed how many of 18 features?

  • A. 0
  • B. 6
  • C. 12
  • D. 18
Answer B. Six weak features (comms quality, temperature, etc.) were removed; 12 retained.

Q7. SVR's epsilon-insensitive loss means:

  • A. Errors smaller than epsilon cost nothing
  • B. All errors are squared
  • C. Only outliers are fitted
  • D. Epsilon is the learning rate
Answer A. \(L_\varepsilon=\max(0,|y-\hat y|-\varepsilon)\): points inside the tube are ignored, giving robustness.

Q8. Gradient boosting for regression fits each new tree to:

  • A. The residuals of the current ensemble
  • B. The original targets
  • C. Random noise
  • D. The test set
Answer A. \(r_i=y_i-F_{m-1}(x_i)\), the negative gradient of squared loss.

Q9. XGBoost validation RMSE was 2.11 h at 400 trees and 2.34 h at 1,200. This shows:

  • A. More trees always help
  • B. Overfitting beyond the optimum: the number of trees is a hyper-parameter (early stopping)
  • C. A bug
  • D. Underfitting
Answer B. Choose M by validation/early stopping.

Q10. RMSE 2.06 h vs MAE 1.54 h suggests:

  • A. No errors
  • B. Negative R-squared
  • C. A few large errors (outlier missions)
  • D. Perfect calibration
Answer C. RMSE weights large errors more; RMSE well above MAE signals heavy-tailed errors.

Q11. R-squared is negative when:

  • A. N is large
  • B. Errors are small
  • C. The model is worse than predicting the mean
  • D. Features are scaled
Answer C. \(R^2=1-SS_{res}/SS_{tot}<0\) when \(SS_{res}>SS_{tot}\).

Q12. Scalar Kalman update: prior variance P = 4, measurement variance R = 1. The gain is:

  • A. 0.2
  • B. 0.25
  • C. 0.8
  • D. 4
Answer C. \(K=P/(P+R)=4/5=0.8\): trust the precise measurement.

Q13. Under Lanchester's square law, Red's per-unit kill rate is four times Blue's. To prevail, Blue needs a force ratio \(B_0/R_0\) greater than:

  • A. 0.5
  • B. 1
  • C. 2
  • D. 4
Answer C. Blue wins if \(bB_0^2>rR_0^2\), i.e. \(B_0/R_0>\sqrt{r/b}=\sqrt4=2\): numbers count squared.

Q14. Hybrid KF+RF fusion kept 94.8% accuracy with 20% IR loss while early-fusion DNN fell to 74.8% because:

  • A. The DNN had fewer features
  • B. IR data are irrelevant
  • C. RF is always better
  • D. The KF handles timing and missing sensors physically; the RF only corrects residual bias
Answer D. Physics backbone plus learned correction is robust to dropout.

Q15. When an IR bearing is imputed, the filter should:

  • A. Treat it exactly as a real measurement
  • B. Set K = 1
  • C. Delete the track
  • D. Flag it and inflate its measurement noise R
Answer D. Otherwise the filter gains false confidence from made-up data.

Part B — Assignment

Instructions. Five problems, 100 marks, plus a 20-mark bonus problem (Problem 6). Show all working. Reference: Lecture 3 reference text.

Problem 1: Least squares by hand 20 marks

EW intensity level $x$ and mission duration $y$ (h) for five missions: (1, 6), (2, 9), (3, 9), (4, 13), (5, 13).

(a) Compute the OLS slope and intercept. [8]
(b) Compute fitted values, residuals, $SS_{res}$, $SS_{tot}$, $R^2$ and RMSE. [8]
(c) Predict duration at EW level 8 and comment on its reliability. [4]

Show full solution

(a) $\bar x=3$, $\bar y=10$. Deviations: $x$: $-2,-1,0,1,2$; $y$: $-4,-1,-1,3,3$. $S_{xy}=8+1+0+3+6=18$, $S_{xx}=10$. Slope $b_1=1.8$ h per level; intercept $b_0=10-1.8(3)=\mathbf{4.6}$ h.

(b) Fitted: 6.4, 8.2, 10.0, 11.8, 13.6. Residuals: $-0.4, 0.8, -1.0, 1.2, -0.6$. $SS_{res}=3.6$; $SS_{tot}=16+1+1+9+9=36$; $R^2=1-3.6/36=\mathbf{0.90}$; RMSE $=\sqrt{3.6/5}=\mathbf{0.85}$ h.

(c) $\hat y=4.6+1.8(8)=19.0$ h, but level 8 is far outside the observed range 1–5: extrapolation assumes linearity continues, and the prediction interval widens sharply ($x^\top(X^\top X)^{-1}x$ grows). Treat it as highly uncertain (a Challenger-style extrapolation risk).

Problem 2: Ridge versus lasso 20 marks

For a single standardised feature, minimise $(z-w)^2+\lambda w^2$ (ridge) or $(z-w)^2+\lambda|w|$ (lasso).

(a) Derive both solutions. [8]
(b) Evaluate both for $\lambda=1$ with $z=2.0$ and $z=0.4$. [6]
(c) Explain which you would use for a 45-feature model in which you suspect only 8 features matter, and why elastic net may be safer. [6]

Show full solution

(a) Ridge: $-2(z-w)+2\lambda w=0\Rightarrow w=z/(1+\lambda)$. Lasso: for $w>0$, $-2(z-w)+\lambda=0\Rightarrow w=z-\lambda/2$ if $z>\lambda/2$; symmetric for $w<0$; otherwise $w=0$. So $w=\text{sign}(z)\max(|z|-\lambda/2,0)$.

(b) $z=2.0$: ridge 1.0, lasso 1.5. $z=0.4$: ridge 0.2, lasso 0 (since $0.4<0.5$). Ridge scales everything by the same factor; lasso subtracts a fixed amount and kills small effects.

(c) Lasso (or elastic net) gives an interpretable sparse model when most features are irrelevant. With correlated groups lasso may keep one feature arbitrarily and drop its partners, and selections can change between samples; elastic net's L2 term spreads weight across correlated features and stabilises selection. Choose penalties by cross-validation.

Problem 3: Two-sensor Kalman fusion 20 marks

Prior range estimate 50 km with variance 9 km². A radar reports 53 km (variance 4); then an IR-derived range reports 49 km (variance 16). Take $H=1$.

(a) Apply the radar update: gain, estimate, variance. [6]
(b) Apply the IR update. [6]
(c) Verify the result with the information form $1/P=\sum1/P_i$, $\hat x=P\sum\hat x_i/P_i$. [4]
(d) Predict to the next step with $F=1$, $Q=1$. [4]

Show full solution

(a) $K=9/13=0.692$; $\hat x=50+0.692(3)=52.08$; $P=(1-0.692)9=2.77$.

(b) $K=2.77/(2.77+16)=0.148$; $\hat x=52.08+0.148(49-52.08)=\mathbf{51.62}$ km; $P=(1-0.148)2.77=\mathbf{2.36}$ km². The noisy IR moves the estimate only slightly.

(c) $1/P=1/9+1/4+1/16=0.4236\Rightarrow P=2.361$. $\hat x=2.361(50/9+53/4+49/16)=2.361(21.87)=51.63$ km. Order of sequential updates does not matter for linear Gaussian fusion.

(d) $\hat x^-=51.62$; $P^-=2.36+1=\mathbf{3.36}$: uncertainty grows between measurements.

Problem 4: Metrics and directional bias 20 marks

Actual mission durations: 10, 12, 20, 8, 30 h. Predictions: 11, 11, 17, 9, 24 h.

(a) Compute MAE, RMSE, signed mean error ($y-\hat y$) and $R^2$. [12]
(b) Interpret for a logistics planner. [8]

Show full solution

(a) Errors $y-\hat y$: $-1, 1, 3, -1, 6$. MAE $=12/5=\mathbf{2.4}$; MSE $=48/5=9.6$, RMSE $=\mathbf{3.10}$; mean error $=8/5=\mathbf{+1.6}$ h; $\bar y=16$, $SS_{tot}=36+16+16+64+196=328$, $R^2=1-48/328=\mathbf{0.854}$.

(b) The model explains most variance but systematically under-predicts by 1.6 h, worst on the longest mission (6 h short). For logistics, under-prediction means running out of fuel or ammunition; add a bias correction or plan on an upper prediction bound rather than the point estimate.

Problem 5: Fusion with imputed data 20 marks

Continue Problem 3 after the radar update ($\hat x=52.08$, $P=2.77$). The IR sensor is blocked by cloud, and an imputation model supplies 49 km.

(a) Compute the update if the imputed value is given the normal IR variance 16. [5]
(b) Recompute with an inflated variance of 64 and compare. [5]
(c) Design a robust three-sensor fusion architecture for Case B, covering timing, missing data, non-linear manoeuvres and validation. [10]

Show full solution

(a) As in Problem 3(b): $\hat x=51.62$, $P=2.36$ --- the filter treats invented data as real and claims extra certainty.

(b) $K=2.77/66.77=0.0415$; $\hat x=52.08-0.0415(3.08)=51.95$; $P=2.65$. The imputed value has little influence and the reported uncertainty stays honest.

(c) A model answer: a hybrid ML-enhanced Kalman (or UKF for manoeuvring targets) per target; asynchronous updates at each sensor's own rate with out-of-sequence handling (roll back and re-propagate); missing sensors skipped rather than imputed, or imputed with inflated $R$ and a flag; an RF residual corrector trained on innovation features; data association gating with the innovation covariance $S$; validation on held-out runs with simulated dropout (e.g. 20% IR), reporting accuracy, latency and calibration of $P$; monitoring for distribution shift in innovations once deployed.

Problem 6 (bonus): Casualty estimates and force ratios 20 marks

A planning cell fits a Poisson model for casualties per company-day, $\ln\mu=0.2+0.3x$, where $x$ is the number of enemy artillery battalions in range. Separately, simulated engagements give Blue losses per hour $\Delta B$ = 4.9, 4.1, 3.3, 2.4 when Red strength $R$ = 120, 100, 80, 60, and Red losses per hour $\Delta R$ = 2.6, 2.3, 2.0, 1.8 when Blue strength $B$ = 90, 80, 70, 60.

(a) For $x=3$, compute $\mu$, the rate ratio per battalion, $P(0)$ and $P(\ge6)$. [6]
(b) Over 40 company-days at $x=3$ the observed variance of casualties is 9.1. Is the Poisson model adequate? Estimate the negative-binomial dispersion $k$ from $\text{Var}=\mu+\mu^2/k$, and say how the planning tail changes ($P(\ge6)$ under the negative binomial is 0.169). [4]
(c) Estimate Red's kill rate $r$ and Blue's kill rate $b$ by regression through the origin. [6]
(d) Blue has 60 units against Red's 50. Under the square law, who prevails, and with how many survivors? Comment on how much you would trust the answer. [4]

Show full solution

(a) $\mu=e^{0.2+0.9}=e^{1.1}=\mathbf{3.00}$. Rate ratio $e^{0.3}=\mathbf{1.35}$ (+35% per battalion). $P(0)=e^{-3.00}=\mathbf{0.050}$. $P(\ge6)=1-P(\le5)=\mathbf{0.084}$.

(b) Dispersion ratio $9.1/3.0=3.0$, far above 1: strongly over-dispersed, so Poisson intervals are too narrow. $k=\mu^2/(\text{Var}-\mu)=9.02/6.10=\mathbf{1.48}$. The probability of six or more casualties doubles, from 0.084 to 0.169: medical and evacuation planning based on the Poisson tail would be badly under-provisioned.

(c) $\hat r=\sum R\,\Delta B/\sum R^2=1{,}406/34{,}400=\mathbf{0.0409}$ Blue units per Red unit per hour. $\hat b=\sum B\,\Delta R/\sum B^2=666/23{,}000=\mathbf{0.0290}$ Red units per Blue unit per hour. Red is about 1.41 times as lethal per unit.

(d) Required ratio $\sqrt{r/b}=\sqrt{1.412}=1.19$; actual $60/50=1.20$. Blue's fighting strength $bB_0^2=104.2$ just exceeds Red's $rR_0^2=102.2$, so Blue prevails — with $\sqrt{60^2-1.412\times50^2}\approx\mathbf{8}$ survivors out of 60. This is a near-tie and a pyrrhic outcome. The coefficients come from four points each, so modest estimation error can reverse the result. Bootstrap or otherwise propagate the uncertainty, check whether the square law fits better than the linear law, and do not present “Blue wins” as a finding.
