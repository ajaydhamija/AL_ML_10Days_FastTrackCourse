Q1. Policy-gradient methods are preferred over DQN for drone thrust control because:

  • A. Continuous actions make argmax over Q an optimisation at every step; a policy can output a distribution directly
  • B. They need no reward
  • C. They are always faster
  • D. They need no exploration
Answer A. A Gaussian policy outputs mean and standard deviation; actions are sampled.

Q2. The policy gradient is \(\mathbb E[\nabla\log\pi_\theta(a|s)\,G_t]\). The term \(\nabla\log\pi\) is called the:

  • A. Advantage
  • B. Entropy
  • C. Score function
  • D. TD error
Answer C. It points in the direction that makes the chosen action more likely.

Q3. REINFORCE's main weakness is:

  • A. Bias
  • B. It needs a model
  • C. High variance from using whole-episode returns
  • D. It cannot handle discrete actions
Answer C. Return spread was 84.6 on the drone task.

Q4. Subtracting a baseline b(s) from the return:

  • A. Biases the gradient
  • B. Removes exploration
  • C. Changes the optimal policy
  • D. Leaves the expected gradient unchanged and reduces variance
Answer D. \(\mathbb E[\nabla\log\pi\,b(s)]=0\).

Q5. A positive advantage A(s,a) means:

  • A. The critic is wrong
  • B. The state is good
  • C. The action was better than average from that state; increase its probability
  • D. Decrease the action's probability
Answer C. \(A=Q-V\).

Q6. In actor-critic, the one-step advantage estimate is:

  • A. \(G_t\)
  • B. \(\log\pi\)
  • C. \(Q(s,a)\)
  • D. \(r+\gamma V(s')-V(s)\)
Answer D. The TD error: lower variance, some bias from an imperfect critic.

Q7. A2C differs from A3C in that A2C:

  • A. Uses no critic
  • B. Synchronises updates from parallel workers (no stale gradients)
  • C. Is off-policy
  • D. Uses a replay buffer
Answer B. A3C updates asynchronously and can suffer from stale gradients.

Q8. PPO clips the probability ratio to \([1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]\) in order to:

  • A. Prevent destructively large policy updates
  • B. Increase exploration
  • C. Remove the critic
  • D. Scale rewards
Answer A. First-order trust-region behaviour without TRPO's second-order cost.

Q9. With \(\varepsilon=0.2\), \(r_t=1.58\), \(\hat A=6.8\), the clipped objective term is:

  • A. 10.76
  • B. 8.16
  • C. 6.8
  • D. 1.58
Answer B. \(\min(1.58\times6.8,1.2\times6.8)=8.16\).

Q10. In a Gaussian policy, the network typically outputs:

  • A. Q-values
  • B. Rewards
  • C. A single deterministic action
  • D. Mean and log-standard-deviation of the action distribution
Answer D. Log-sigma keeps sigma positive and stabilises training.

Q11. SAC's objective adds which term to the reward?

  • A. A KL penalty
  • B. A clip
  • C. A baseline
  • D. \(\alpha H(\pi(\cdot|s))\), the policy entropy
Answer D. Maximum-entropy RL: explore, stay robust, stay unpredictable.

Q12. Under wind perturbation PPO fell to 71% while SAC held 88%. The best explanation is:

  • A. SAC's entropy term learns many ways to succeed, so it is robust to disturbances
  • B. SAC used more data
  • C. PPO is broken
  • D. Wind affects PPO only
Answer A. Stochastic, diverse behaviour generalises better to perturbations.

Q13. SAC is off-policy, which means:

  • A. It needs complete episodes
  • B. It never explores
  • C. It learns from a replay buffer of past experience, reusing data
  • D. It has no critic
Answer C. Hence high sample efficiency per environment interaction.

Q14. Drone success fell from 94% in simulation to 78% in the field. The recommended remedy is:

  • A. Train longer in the same simulator
  • B. Domain randomisation over wind, noise, mass and motor variations
  • C. Use REINFORCE
  • D. Lower gamma
Answer B. With randomisation real-world success rose to 88% (PPO) and 91% (SAC).

Q15. The SAC drones kept about 32.4 m spacing without being told. This illustrates:

  • A. Overfitting
  • B. Emergent coordinated behaviour from reward plus entropy
  • C. A bug
  • D. Reward hacking
Answer B. Spacing between the 10 m penalty and 50 m bonus emerged naturally.

Part B — Assignment

Instructions. Five problems, 100 marks, plus a 20-mark bonus problem (Problem 6). Reference: Lecture 7 reference text.

Problem 1: REINFORCE by hand 20 marks

A softmax policy over {left, right} has preferences $h=(0,0)$; $\nabla_h\log\pi(a)=\mathbf 1_a-\pi$.

(a) The agent takes right and receives return $G=4$; with $\alpha=0.1$ compute the new preferences and $\pi(\text{right})$. [8]
(b) Repeat from the original policy with a baseline $b=3$. [6]
(c) Explain why the baseline version is preferable when returns range between 0 and 8. [6]

Show full solution

(a) $\nabla\log\pi(\text{right})=(-0.5,0.5)$. $h\leftarrow0.1\times4\times(-0.5,0.5)=(-0.2,0.2)$; $\pi(\text{right})=e^{0.2}/(e^{0.2}+e^{-0.2})=\mathbf{0.60}$.

(b) Effective weight $G-b=1$: $h=(-0.05,0.05)$; $\pi(\text{right})=e^{0.05}/(e^{0.05}+e^{-0.05})=\mathbf{0.525}$.

(c) Without a baseline every action is reinforced (all returns are positive) and only the relative size of updates carries information, so variance is high. With a baseline near the mean return, below-average outcomes push probability down and above-average ones push it up; updates are smaller and better-signed, and the expected gradient is unchanged.

Problem 2: The baseline proof and advantages 20 marks

(a) Prove $\mathbb E_{a\sim\pi}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a|s)\,b(s)]=0$. [8]
(b) Why must the baseline not depend on the action? [4]
(c) A critic gives $V(s)=10$, $V(s')=12$; reward $r=1$, $\gamma=0.9$. Compute the one-step advantage, and explain the sign. [8]

Show full solution

(a) $\sum_a\pi(a|s)\frac{\nabla\pi(a|s)}{\pi(a|s)}b(s)=b(s)\nabla\sum_a\pi(a|s)=b(s)\nabla1=0$.

(b) If $b$ depended on $a$ it could not be taken outside the sum, the identity would fail, and the gradient would be biased.

(c) $\hat A=1+0.9\times12-10=\mathbf{1.8}$. The transition led to a state worth more than expected, so the action was better than average and its probability is increased.

Problem 3: PPO clipping 20 marks

$\varepsilon=0.2$. Compute the clipped-objective term and state whether its gradient with respect to $r$ is zero, for: (i) $r=1.1,\hat A=5$; (ii) $r=1.5,\hat A=5$; (iii) $r=0.9,\hat A=-3$; (iv) $r=0.6,\hat A=-3$; (v) $r=1.5,\hat A=-3$. [15] Explain case (v). [5]

Show full solution

(i) $\min(5.5,5.5)=5.5$, gradient non-zero. (ii) $\min(7.5,6.0)=\mathbf{6.0}$, clipped, zero gradient. (iii) $\min(-2.7,-2.7)=-2.7$, non-zero. (iv) $\min(-1.8,-2.4)=\mathbf{-2.4}$, clipped, zero gradient. (v) $\min(-4.5,-3.6)=\mathbf{-4.5}$, not clipped.

Case (v): a bad action has become much more likely. The min keeps the unclipped, more pessimistic term, so the gradient still pushes its probability down. PPO clips only in the direction that would over-reward a change, never in the direction that corrects a mistake.

Problem 4: Gaussian policy update 20 marks

A 1-D thrust policy has $\mu=2.0$, $\sigma=0.5$.

(a) Sampled $a=2.4$, advantage $+3$, step size 0.01: update $\mu$. [6]
(b) Sampled $a=1.5$, advantage $-2$: update $\mu$ from 2.0. [6]
(c) Derive $\partial\log\pi/\partial\sigma$ and explain how $\sigma$ changes when an action far from the mean has positive advantage. [8]

Show full solution

(a) $\partial\log\pi/\partial\mu=(a-\mu)/\sigma^2=1.6$; $\mu=2.0+0.01\times3\times1.6=\mathbf{2.048}$.

(b) $(1.5-2.0)/0.25=-2$; times $\hat A=-2$ gives $+4$; $\mu=2.0+0.04=\mathbf{2.04}$ — away from the bad action.

(c) $\log\pi=-\frac{(a-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}-\log\sigma+\text{const}$, so $\partial/\partial\sigma=\frac{(a-\mu)^2}{\sigma^3}-\frac1\sigma$. It is positive when $|a-\mu|>\sigma$. A distant action with positive advantage therefore increases $\sigma$ (explore more widely); a near-mean success decreases it (act more precisely).

Problem 5: Choosing and validating a controller 20 marks

You must field an autonomous waypoint controller for a 4-drone ISR swarm.

(a) Compare PPO and SAC on five criteria and recommend one. [8]
(b) Design a domain-randomisation scheme and a field-validation protocol. [8]
(c) What safety architecture would you place around the learned policy? [4]

Show full solution

(a) Criteria: sample efficiency (SAC off-policy reuse vs PPO on-policy); robustness (SAC 88% vs PPO 71% in wind); stability and tuning effort (PPO simpler); predictability for certification (PPO near-deterministic; SAC must anneal its stochasticity); adversarial unpredictability (SAC better). For a contested ISR mission where robustness matters and simulation is cheap, SAC with entropy annealed at deployment, keeping PPO as a baseline.

(b) Randomise wind (0–8 m/s, gusts), sensor and GPS noise ($\pm2$ m), mass ($\pm15$%), motor coefficients ($\pm10$%), latency (0–50 ms) and battery sag. Validate in hardware-in-the-loop, then tethered and range trials across weather, reporting success rate with confidence intervals over many sorties and a pre-set acceptance threshold.

(c) A certified PID inner loop; geofence and minimum-separation enforcement outside the learned policy; a fallback controller and return-to-home on anomaly; human override and full logging. The learned policy proposes waypoints and thrust targets within a verified safety envelope.

Problem 6 (bonus): Policy-gradient updates by hand 20 marks

(a) A climb/hold policy has preferences $(0.2,-0.2)$. The agent climbed and the episode return was $G=10$; the baseline is $b=6$. With $\alpha=0.05$, update the preferences and the probabilities. Repeat without the baseline and comment. [6]
(b) A Gaussian thrust policy has $\mu=0.40$, $\sigma=0.10$; it sampled $a=0.47$ and the advantage was $+3$. Compute the gradients with respect to $\mu$ and $\ln\sigma$, and the new $\mu$ with $\alpha=0.001$. [5]
(c) PPO with $\varepsilon=0.2$: three samples have $(r_t,\hat A_t)=(1.35,+2.0)$, $(0.70,-1.5)$ and $(1.10,-0.8)$. Compute each clipped term and their mean. Which samples contribute no gradient? [6]
(d) Rewards $1,0,5$ over three steps that end the episode; value estimates $V=2.0,2.5,3.0$; $\gamma=0.99$, $\lambda=0.95$. Compute the GAE advantages. [3]

Show full solution

(a) $\pi=(0.599,0.401)$; score $(1-0.599,\;-0.401)=(0.401,-0.401)$. With advantage $G-b=4$: preferences change by $0.05\times4\times(0.401,-0.401)$ to $(0.280,-0.280)$, giving $\pi=(\mathbf{0.637},0.363)$. Without the baseline (multiplier 10): $\pi(\text{climb})=0.690$. The direction is the same, but the step is 2.5 times larger. Because every return is positive, an unbaselined update would push up whatever action was taken; the baseline credits climbing only for doing better than usual, which cuts variance.

(b) $\partial\ln\pi/\partial\mu=(0.47-0.40)/0.01=7$; times $A=3$: $\mathbf{21}$, so $\mu\to0.40+0.001\times21=\mathbf{0.421}$. $\partial\ln\pi/\partial\ln\sigma=(0.07^2/0.01-1)=-0.51$; times 3: $\mathbf{-1.53}$, so $\sigma$ shrinks — a good action close to the mean narrows the distribution.

(c) Sample 1: $\min(1.35\times2,\;1.2\times2)=\mathbf{2.4}$ (clipped). Sample 2: $\min(0.70\times-1.5,\;0.8\times-1.5)=\min(-1.05,-1.2)=\mathbf{-1.2}$ (clipped). Sample 3: $r$ is inside $[0.8,1.2]$, so the term is $\mathbf{-0.88}$. Mean $=\mathbf{0.107}$. Samples 1 and 2 take the clipped constant, so they contribute no gradient: the policy has already moved as far as PPO allows in those directions. Only sample 3 drives the update.

(d) TD errors: $\delta_0=1+0.99(2.5)-2=1.475$; $\delta_1=0+0.99(3)-2.5=0.470$; $\delta_2=5+0-3=2.0$. With $\gamma\lambda=0.9405$: $\hat A_2=\mathbf{2.00}$, $\hat A_1=0.470+0.9405(2.0)=\mathbf{2.35}$, $\hat A_0=1.475+0.9405(2.351)=\mathbf{3.69}$.
